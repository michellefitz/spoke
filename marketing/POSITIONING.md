# Spoke — positioning & voice

The grounding doc for all Spoke marketing. Short on purpose. If a piece of copy
doesn't fit this, rewrite the copy — or argue with this doc and update it.

## The market in five lines

1. **A commodity tier exploded in late 2025**: dozens of near-identical indie
   "speak → organized list" iOS apps (Voice Do, Tudo, SmartLists, VoiceTask,
   Akord…), all saying the same thing: "capture instantly", "organized in
   seconds". We cannot win by saying those words too.
2. **Todoist shipped Ramble** (GA Jan 2026, 76k users in 3 weeks): real-time
   voice capture inside a heavyweight typed-first app. It validates the
   behavior and commodifies bare capture. Its documented weaknesses: ~62%
   task-creation success, it dumps raw thoughts you still have to process
   later, and Doist explicitly positions it as "a quick capture tool, not a
   conversational assistant."
3. **Superlist Talk** paywalls voice capture at $5/mo, aimed at teams.
4. **An ADHD-branded cluster** (Blurto, BrainPal, Flow Copilot, Sprout) proves
   the executive-dysfunction segment is real; most are new/waitlist-stage.
5. **General assistants** (ChatGPT/Claude voice) parse a braindump brilliantly
   and then have nowhere to put it — bullet points in a chat, or a clumsy
   handoff to Reminders.

## What only Spoke can say

- **The whole app is the conversation.** Voice isn't a mic button bolted onto
  a form-based app — it's the only way in, and the app talks back: it sums up
  what it heard, and asks one question when it isn't sure.
- **You get a finished list, not homework.** Ramble hands you raw thoughts to
  triage later. Spoke hands you titled, sorted, dated tasks.
- **No fake deadlines.** "This week" and "next week" are real due dates in
  Spoke. Every other app forces a day on you, then punishes you in red when
  you miss the day you never really meant. Nobody else in the space says this.
- **A person made this.** One builder, for her own chaotic list. The commodity
  tier is template marketing; sounding human is a feature they can't ship.

## Who it's for

**Primary: mental-load carriers.** Busy parents — disproportionately mothers —
running the household project-management layer in their heads ("the running
to-do list in your brain that never shuts off"; studies put ~73% of household
cognitive work on mothers). Their moment: hands full, brain fuller, no chance
to sit down and type into forms. Founder-market fit is exact — write to this
person as a peer, because she is one.

**Secondary: ADHD / executive-dysfunction folks.** Highest intent, evangelical,
and they already have the vocabulary: *brain dump, task paralysis, out of my
head, the app became another task to maintain, the perfect-system trap.* We
speak their language and solve their problem — but we don't medicalize the
brand or claim to be "an ADHD app". They'll recognize themselves.

**Tertiary: think-out-loud people** — walkers, drivers, commuters who narrate
their day anyway.

## Why people abandon to-do apps (message against these)

- "The app became another task to maintain." → Spoke has no system to set up.
- Entry friction: forms, fields, dates, priorities per item. → You just talk.
- Fake precision: forced dates → overdue guilt → deleted app. → Soft weeks.
- Over-featured: projects, labels, filters, karma. → A list and a mic.

## Positioning statement

For people whose to-do list lives in their head and won't shut up, **Spoke is
the to-do list you talk to.** Say everything out loud — messy, out of order,
half-formed — and it comes back as a clean list: titles, subtasks, the right
days, and honest "this week"s for the things that don't have a day. If it's
not sure, it asks. Unlike to-do apps with a mic bolted on, the whole thing is
built around how you actually think: out loud.

**Working taglines** (in order of preference):
1. "Say it, we'll sort it." *(already in onboarding — keep, it's the truest)*
2. "The to-do list you talk to."
3. "Get it out of your head."

*("Your day, dictated." is polished but reads like adland — demote or drop.)*

## Tone of voice

**The test: read it aloud. If Michelle wouldn't say it to a friend at the
kitchen table, rewrite it.** It's a speech app; the copy should sound like
speech.

Principles:
- Talk to one person, in second person, with contractions and short sentences.
- Concrete beats abstract: name real tasks ("book the dentist", "bins out",
  "party prep"), never "boost your productivity".
- The AI is quiet staff, not the star. Describe what it does ("it'll ask if
  it's not sure"), never how impressive it is.
- Honest and a little wry. Admit limits. Under-promise.
- Sell the feeling, and the feeling is *relief* — it's out of your head and
  it's handled.

**Do:** everyday examples · "you" · verbs · the relief moment · one idea per
sentence · sound like a text message from a competent friend.

**Don't:** "AI-powered" as a selling point · effortless / seamless /
supercharge / unlock / unleash / revolutionize · "productivity" as a goal ·
exclamation marks · feature laundry lists · anything that could appear in
another app's listing unchanged.

**Rewrites (calibration examples):**
- ~~"Press. Talk. Done. Spoke turns your words into clean, readable tasks —
  no reformatting required."~~ → "Tap the mic and say it all — the school
  thing, the bins, the dentist. You get back a list you'd have written
  yourself, if you ever got a minute."
- ~~"Just talk. Spoke turns your words into organized tasks."~~ → "Just talk.
  It sorts out what you said into a proper list."
- Keep: "Say it, we'll sort it."

## Monetization (parked, on purpose)

Free while we find real users. No paywall until people demonstrably rely on
it. Ideas exist; implementing them now is premature.
